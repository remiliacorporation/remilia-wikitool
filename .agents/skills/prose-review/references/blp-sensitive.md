# Living-person and sensitive-claim review

Apply this review conservatively to living or plausibly living people and to claims about health, drugs, crime, arrests, lawsuits, finances, sexuality, identity, abuse, harassment, extremist affiliation, professional misconduct, private relationships, or reputational controversy.

## Per-claim requirements

For every sensitive claim:

- identify the exact proposition and who it concerns;
- inspect the exact source passage;
- prefer high-quality reporting, official records within their scope, or direct on-record statements;
- require explicit attribution for allegations, opinion, diagnosis, or contested characterization;
- preserve legal status and time: allegation, charge, plea, conviction, dismissal, settlement, or later correction are not interchangeable;
- avoid inference from images, usernames, social graphs, transaction proximity, deleted posts, or community repetition;
- confirm that inclusion is necessary and proportionate to an encyclopedic account.

## Immediate blockers

Block publication when:

- a sensitive claim has no direct citation;
- the citation is a tertiary summary, search snippet, generic home page, anonymous low-control source, or unrelated article;
- the prose is more certain or broader than the source;
- several sensitive claims share one citation that supports only part of them;
- identity is uncertain or conflated;
- private information is unnecessary to the article;
- a correction, acquittal, dismissal, or material update is omitted;
- the only rationale for inclusion is notoriety, shock, or a local relationship.

## Tone and placement

Use precise, attributed language. Avoid euphemism that obscures the record and sensationalism that amplifies it. Do not place a sensitive detail in the lead unless it is central to the subject's documented significance and due weight clearly supports that prominence.

## Absence and omission

Omitting an inadequately sourced sensitive claim is preferable to weakening its wording until it sounds harmless. Record the held claim and missing evidence outside publishable prose so it can be revisited.

Human acceptance does not cure an evidence defect. A clean mechanical lint report does not reduce BLP risk.
