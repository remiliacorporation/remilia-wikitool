# Remilia Wiki visual-subject supplement

Use this supplement for artwork, character designs, NFT/PFP collections, cards, logos, comics, and other primarily visual subjects.

## Artifact description

A work displayed in the article is a primary artifact. After actually viewing it, an author may describe plainly visible subject matter, composition, pose, framing, color, medium when unambiguous, printed text, and recurring features. The description must be independently checkable against the shown work.

The artifact does not by itself establish intent, symbolism, influence, authorship, production process, release date, reception, popularity, supply, or significance. Those require another inspected source or an attributed durable creator statement. A user's verbal description is a source lead until the agent views the artifact.

Show before describing. Captions identify and locate; they should not praise or interpret. Describe load-bearing distinctions rather than cataloguing every visible detail.

## Remilia-specific authorship

Where post-authorship applies, attribute a series to Remilia or the sourced collective entity rather than inventing an individual author. Name contributors only when an inspected source credits them. Do not infer an individual author from style, access, social proximity, or local knowledge.

Terms such as network spirituality, dollcore, oekaki, and gijinka can be recorded as terminology. Claiming that a particular work belongs to, derives from, or intentionally references one of these traditions is interpretive and needs a source or attributed creator statement.

## Structure and thinness

Potential sections include design and aesthetic, production, series or roster, influence and lineage, and reception. Include only sections supported by the artifact and source packet. If visual description yields one small paragraph and the rest would be inferred context, prefer a redirect or a section in a parent article.

Avoid art-criticism puffery, unsourced resemblance, inferred process, and intent attribution. A shorter description that the reader can verify is stronger than a speculative interpretation.
