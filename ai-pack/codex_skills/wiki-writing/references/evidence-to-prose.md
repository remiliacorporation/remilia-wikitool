# Evidence to prose

## Evidence classes

Treat these as evidence only after inspecting the underlying material:

- an original statement, document, artwork, transaction, repository, recording, or other primary artifact for what it directly shows;
- an authoritative institutional record for the facts within its remit;
- an independent secondary source for reporting, contextualization, reception, criticism, or significance;
- a human interview statement as attributed firsthand knowledge or as a lead to verify, according to the publication's policy.

These are never evidence by themselves:

- model memory;
- search-result snippets;
- retrieval scores or semantic neighbors;
- another wiki's prose;
- citation metadata without the source body;
- a fluent synthesis of multiple uninspected claims.

## Claim-source map

Make the map at claim granularity, not paragraph granularity. A practical row contains:

| Planned claim | Exact source | Entailing passage or record | Source role | Qualification or hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Split a sentence when its clauses require different sources. One citation at the end of a dense paragraph is not enough when the source supports only one clause.

## Source fidelity

- Cite the document actually inspected. Do not cite a home page, publisher root, search result, or stronger-looking substitute.
- Preserve what the source says and what it does not say. Similarity is not entailment.
- Keep dates, quantities, identities, quotations, uncertainty, and causal direction exact.
- Attribute opinion, analysis, interpretation, allegations, and contested labels.
- Do not turn absence of evidence into evidence of absence.
- Do not turn chronology into causation.
- Do not use one source to imply broad consensus.

## Wikipedia and other tertiary pages

Use tertiary pages to locate candidate sources, alternative names, and coverage questions. Follow their citations to the underlying documents and inspect those documents. Never compress a tertiary article's many citations into one citation to the tertiary page or to an unrelated publisher home page.

If the underlying source cannot be inspected, omit or hold the claim. A direct citation to another wiki may be appropriate only when that wiki page itself is the subject or primary artifact being discussed, and the prose makes that role explicit.

## Primary and secondary sources

Primary sources are often best for dates, releases, stated intentions, technical properties, and the content of a work. They do not independently establish importance, reception, controversy, or the truth of self-serving claims.

Secondary sources are required when prose synthesizes significance or situates a subject in a broader history. Prefer sources with demonstrated editorial control and direct reporting. Evaluate conflicts of interest and proximity to the subject.

## Quotations

Quote only when the exact wording is itself useful. Verify the words against the source, keep context, identify the speaker, and cite the source that contains the quotation. Paraphrase routine facts. Do not assemble a paragraph around ornamental quotations.

## Failure patterns that block drafting

- a central fact is supported only by a search snippet or another wiki;
- one generic citation is made to carry unrelated criminal, medical, biographical, or reputational claims;
- a source URL does not point to the cited work;
- a compressed paraphrase changes dates, actors, quantities, or causal claims;
- a relationship to the host wiki is inferred from local adjacency rather than sourced importance;
- the prose states significance because coverage exists, without a source establishing significance.
